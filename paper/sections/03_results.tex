\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Benchmark setup}
\curia{} was evaluated on 19 human-to-query genome pairs, using human (hg38) as
the reference, spanning primates through the marsupial
outgroups: rhesus macaque (rheMac10), mouse (mm39), rat (rn7), rabbit (HLoryCun3),
cow (bosTau9), pig (susScr11), Bryde's whale (HLbalEde1), dromedary (HLcamDro2),
horse (equCab3), cat (felCat9), dog (canFam5), pangolin (HLmanJav2), flying fox
(HLpteVam2), hedgehog (eriEur2), armadillo (dasNov3), Asian elephant (HLeleMax1),
and three marsupials: gray short-tailed opossum (monDom5), Virginia opossum
(HLdidVir1), and
tammar wallaby (HLnotEug3). Genome assemblies
were obtained from NCBI \citep{sayersDatabaseResourcesNational2022} and the UCSC
Genome Browser \citep{kentHumanGenomeBrowser2002}. Due to variability in
annotation quality across species, high-quality ncRNA annotations were used only
for human (GENCODE V48) and mouse (GENCODE VM37)
\citep{harrowGENCODEReferenceHuman2012}, while other species were analyzed without
relying on their native annotations. Human annotation contains thousands of
lncRNA loci, but biological evidence and mechanistic characterization are
concentrated in a much smaller subset; the remainder comprises a mixture of
functional transcripts, transcription-associated products, and uncertain
annotations \citep{statelloGeneRegulationLong2021,mattickLongNoncodingRNAs2023}.
Evaluation was performed at both the locus level (for
short ncRNAs and lncRNA loci) and the substructure level (for island matches),
following the criteria defined in Section~\ref{sec:eval}.

\subsection{Overall performance}
We first quantified short-branch processing yield and agreement between predicted
loci and existing annotations. Of 9{,}247 annotated non-protein-coding human union
loci satisfying the single-exon, $\leq256$-nt short-branch geometry, 2{,}949 were
assigned at least one chain-projected mouse candidate locus and 2{,}948 produced a
refined prediction. Multiple retained chains produced 2{,}994 prediction rows,
which form the denominator for the following query-annotation agreement analysis;
these counts measure workflow coverage, not recall against known orthologs. Among
the 2{,}994 predictions,
52.4\% show any overlap with an annotated exon ($\geq 1$\,nt; any-overlap rate),
46.5\% have $\geq 50\%$ of the predicted-locus length covered by an annotated
exon, and 26.0\% have $\geq 99\%$ prediction coverage. These are one-directional
prediction-coverage criteria, not reciprocal overlap. Among any-overlap predictions, the median overlap
is 98.9\% (mean 86.8\%), indicating that refined predicted loci are typically well
localized and consistent with annotated boundaries.

Under the conservative exact-name criterion, 446 geometry-eligible human loci had
an annotated mouse gene with the same name; \curia{} predictions overlapped an
exon of that same-name mouse gene for 373 loci (83.6\% rough named-counterpart
recall). Because this subset is enriched for established, consistently named RNA
families, it should not be interpreted as recall over all short ncRNAs.

Refined predicted loci are generally well localized when annotation support is
present.

\subsection{Short ncRNA performance and score behavior}
% RiNALMo human-mouse numbers (analysis/pair_numbers.py + compute_fig4_data.py).
We evaluated similarity between matched short ncRNA loci using MMD in embedding
space. For the human--mouse pair (2{,}994 loci), the MMD distribution is skewed
toward low values (median 0.142; Fig.~\ref{fig:mmd}A), with a substantial fraction
of near-zero values (32.5\% below 0.05), indicating consistent embedding
representations between matched reference and query loci.

Stratification by biotype reveals clear differences (Fig.~\ref{fig:mmd}C).
snoRNAs and tRNAs exhibit the lowest MMD values (median $\approx 0$), consistent
with their strong, well-characterized structural conservation
\citep{chanTRNAscanSE20Improved2021}. miRNAs and lncRNAs occupy an intermediate
range, whereas snRNAs, misc\_RNAs, and rRNA-derived loci show higher and broader
distributions. The rRNA category is too small and heterogeneous here for a
biological conclusion about rRNA conservation; its distribution may instead
reflect copy assignment, pseudogene contamination, projection, or boundary
effects.

MMD is strongly associated with annotation overlap. Predictions with low MMD
values ($\lesssim 0.1$) show high any-overlap rates with annotated loci
(${\sim}88\%$),
whereas higher MMD values show reduced overlap and increased dispersion.
Predictions with MMD $> 0.2$ rarely overlap annotations and are enriched for
ambiguous cases. Consistent with this, loci overlapping annotations are enriched
at low MMD values, while non-overlapping predictions are shifted toward higher
values (Fig.~\ref{fig:mmd}B), supporting the interpretation of MMD as a continuous
empirical association with annotation support in this benchmark, rather than a
globally calibrated confidence scale.

A subset of predictions with very low reported MMD does not overlap existing
annotations. Zero is the minimum reported score but can result from clipping a
negative unbiased estimate.

Embedding similarity was strongly associated with nucleotide similarity but was
not fully reducible to the tested sequence-derived metrics. Across all 2{,}994
short-ncRNA predictions, MMD was anticorrelated with nucleotide identity (Pearson
$r=-0.80$; Spearman $\rho=-0.85$). A multivariate sequence-only model predicted
annotation support with ROC-AUC 0.887, compared with 0.893 after adding MMD
($\Delta$ROC-AUC $=+0.0064$, 95\% CI $[0.0030,0.0097]$). This small increment
disappeared when miRNAs were excluded ($\Delta=-0.0007$), indicating that MMD adds
little predictive information over the full sequence baseline for the remaining
short-ncRNA classes in this benchmark.

\subsection{Localized embedding-supported regions (``islands'')}
% Island match metric is embedding-SW distance d = 1/(1 + score), not MMD.
We analyzed localized subregions (``islands'') within lncRNAs to assess
cross-species correspondence beyond the locus level. Islands are matched at
token level by Smith--Waterman local alignment over the per-token cosine
similarity dotplot, and match quality is reported as an embedding-SW distance
$d = 1 / (1 + \mathrm{score})$ (lower is better; Section~\ref{sec:islandalign}). Island
detection reveals that detector-positive embedding patterns are sparse and localized.
Across the 19 comparisons, 34{,}532 of 34{,}846 annotated human lncRNA union loci
entered reference-island processing in at least one comparison, and 18{,}364
(53.2\%) had at least one reference island. Among island-positive loci, the mean
was 1.51 reference islands and the median was 1 (27{,}784 detector islands in
total), indicating a small number of localized regions rather than signal
distributed uniformly across the transcript (Fig.~\ref{fig:cores}A). Because an
island can cross splice junctions, the 27{,}784 islands comprise 43{,}506 genomic
segments.

To assess cross-species correspondence, we evaluated matches between reference and
query islands. About half of
matched islands (50.1\%) overlap annotated ncRNA regions in the query genome, and
28.6\% show $\geq 50\%$ exonic overlap.

Together, these observations support a modular pattern of cross-species
correspondence, with signal concentrated in discrete localized regions rather than
uniformly distributed across entire transcripts
\citep{hezroniPrinciplesLongNoncoding2015,rivasEvolutionaryConservationRNA2021,ulitskyEvolutionRescueUsing2016,mattickLongNoncodingRNAs2023,quinnUniqueFeaturesLong2016}.
This interpretation is broadly consistent with observations from well-characterized
lncRNAs such as NEAT1, XIST, and MALAT1, in which localized domains have been
linked to distinct architectural, spreading, or stability-related functions
\citep{wiluszTripleHelixStabilizes2012,yamazakiFunctionalDomainsNEAT12018,engreitzXistLncRNAExploits2013}.

\subsubsection{Multi-species recurrent cores}
Here, ``core'' refers operationally to an island showing recurrent
accepted matches across multiple species within syntenically projected
loci. We observe a subset of islands that exhibit reproducible cross-species
matches (Fig.~\ref{fig:cores}B--C). Figure~\ref{fig:cores}B starts from 31{,}977
reference-coordinate cores having at least one accepted match in any query genome;
it therefore excludes loci without a detected reference island and islands with
no accepted match. Within this conditional denominator, 87\% are recovered in at least
two query genomes, 67\% in five or more query genomes, and approximately 2\% are
detected across all 19 query genomes, including the marsupial outgroups (opossums and
wallaby). An absent species in this aggregation can reflect either missing
projection or failure to obtain an accepted match; the bar plot does not separate
those outcomes.
The two counts use different universes. The 27{,}784 detector islands are unique
islands detected in human lncRNA loci before projection. The 31{,}977 cores are
reconstructed from accepted match rows across all 19 species runs and all
island-branch biotypes; species-run-specific genomic segments are subsequently
aggregated by overlap in human reference coordinates.

In contrast to locus-level overlap, which is often incomplete or ambiguous, these
cores exhibit recurrent cross-species correspondence at the level of localized
embedding similarity. To assess whether such matches could arise from selecting
the best of many candidate positions within a syntenic window, we compared each
matched query island against same-length windows tiled across its own projected
syntenic locus (a within-locus positional null; representative genome pairs). The
assigned match showed strong cross-locus specificity: among 250 pairs per
comparison, the assigned query ranked in the top 5\% for 69\% of human--cow and
65\% of human--mouse reference islands, and assigned-versus-cross-locus AUCs were
0.907 and 0.898, respectively. Assigned pairs were closer than their
dinucleotide-preserving query shuffles in 94\% and 92\% of cases. In the
within-locus analysis, 287 cow and 308 mouse loci contained at least one
non-overlapping alternative. The assigned interval outscored every such
alternative in 189/287 (65.9\%) and 202/308 (65.6\%) loci, compared with mean
locus-specific chance probabilities of 15.6\% and 15.9\%. This enrichment remained
well above chance in strata containing 20 or more alternatives (50\% versus
approximately 3\% for cow and 58\% versus approximately 3\% for mouse). These
controls provide evidence against cross-locus, composition-only, and simple
best-of-many positional artifacts in the tested loci, without establishing that
every matched island is functional. Although substantially above the
locus-specific chance expectation, the assigned interval was not the top-scoring
non-overlapping alternative in approximately one third of evaluable loci,
indicating substantial residual positional ambiguity.

A complementary score-reducibility analysis asked whether the island score adds
ranking information beyond nucleotide identity, local sequence alignment, k-mer
similarity, composition, and length. Adding embedding-SW to the multivariate
sequence-only baseline improved discrimination of CURIA-assigned partners from
cross-locus alternatives in both tested samples: ROC-AUC increased from 0.897 to
0.935 for human--mouse ($\Delta=+0.038$, 95\% cluster-bootstrap CI
$[0.022,0.056]$) and from 0.912 to 0.952 for human--cow ($\Delta=+0.040$,
$[0.026,0.055]$). In the prespecified low-sequence-similarity subset,
embedding-SW retained substantially stronger discrimination than nucleotide
Smith--Waterman (AUC 0.798 versus 0.615 for human--mouse and 0.796 versus 0.616
for human--cow). Within the same syntenic locus, embedding-SW also ranked the
pipeline-selected match first more often than nucleotide Smith--Waterman (31.0\%
versus 18.4\% for human--mouse; 24.4\% versus 11.6\% for human--cow). This
assignment-reproduction analysis evaluates recovery of the CURIA-assigned partner, not whether that
partner is the true biological ortholog.

\subsection{Near-panel-wide recurrent cores and coding proximity}
The 1{,}693-locus result follows a defined funnel. The reference contains
34{,}846 annotated human lncRNA union gene loci; 34{,}532 entered the island branch
in at least one genome comparison, 18{,}364 had at least one reference island, and
11{,}328 of those were projectable in at least 17 query genomes. Requiring an
accepted match for the same reference-coordinate core in $\geq17$ genomes retained
1{,}761 annotated lncRNA gene loci before reference-length filtering and 1{,}693
after requiring a mean reference-island length of at least 120\,bp. The historical
aggregate mean-distance cutoff of 0.10 adds no independent stringency because the
individual accepted rows are already bounded at $d\leq0.10$. The final stages are aggregated by versionless ENSG identifier;
thus, 1{,}693 counts gene loci, not individual islands or de novo lncRNA annotations.
This operational
threshold allows two biological or technical non-detections; failure to recover a
core is not by itself interpreted as lineage-specific absence. Of these loci, 379
are intergenic relative to human protein-coding annotations (no overlap and more
than 5\,kb from a coding gene). The
full set of loci (all conservation categories) is provided as a supplementary
table in the repository:
\url{https://github.com/kirilenkobm/CURIA_pipeline/blob/master/paper/lncRNAs_with_conserved_cores.tsv}.
These loci
represent candidate recurrent cores within known human lncRNA loci and may include
false positives despite filtering.
The intergenic subset shows that the screen is not restricted to direct overlap
with protein-coding genes, although conservation of other DNA-level regulatory or
repetitive elements remains a possible confounder.

Because the matcher accepts island matches at embedding-SW distance $d\leq0.10$,
the broad threshold
primarily defines recurrence and coverage rather than stringent per-species match
quality. We therefore repeated the 17-of-19 quorum analysis while imposing the
same stricter distance cutoff in every counted species. The broad set contained
1{,}693 loci; requiring $d\leq0.05$, $0.03$, and $0.02$ retained 784, 379, and
170 loci, respectively, including 187, 103, and 42 intergenic loci. Even the
ultra-stringent $d\leq0.01$ cutoff retained 21 loci. These nested subsets show
that the recurrent signal is not confined to matches near the acceptance ceiling,
while also making clear that the broad set is substantially larger than the
high-confidence subsets.

\subsection{Case studies}
To illustrate the behavior of \curia{}, we examined individual loci
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cases}). Projection of human SNORD57 to mouse identifies a match
precisely localized over annotated Snord57, illustrating recovery of a strongly
conserved compact ncRNA. Projection of human RNU6-7 intersects an
annotated mouse locus (Gm24859). Projection of human vault RNA
(ENSG00000199990) identifies a corresponding locus overlapping mouse Vaultrc5; the
matched region differs in length from the human transcript, yet \curia{} localizes
the most similar segment within the syntenic region. Quantitatively, the SNORD57
projection overlaps annotated mouse Snord57 at 74\% edit-derived identity
(reported MMD 0.00), and RNU6-7 overlaps Gm24859 at 93\% edit-derived identity (reported MMD
0.00). The vault-RNA projection overlaps Vaultrc5 despite lower sequence
similarity (59\% edit-derived identity; normalized nucleotide-SW
score 0.35; MMD 0.14), illustrating localization across examples spanning a
broad range of sequence conservation.

\subsection{Exploratory comparison with predicted secondary structure}
We compared the representative short-ncRNA intervals and four accepted MALAT1
recurrent-core matches with alignment-aware ViennaRNA predictions. RNU6-7, used
here as a positive control, showed near-identical predicted pairing after mapping
the human and mouse base-pair probability matrices into shared alignment
coordinates, whereas SNORD57 showed partial agreement. Vault RNA exhibited
prominent long-range pairing in both predicted folds but weak alignment-aware
contact correspondence. Among the MALAT1 examples, R5 showed the strongest and
most stable moderate agreement across the tested boundaries and folding
procedures; R4 was weak and boundary-dependent, R3 was weak and inconsistent
between global and local folding, and R2 was weak and near background
(Supplementary Figures S1--S7 and Supplementary Table S1). These heterogeneous
outcomes indicate that embedding similarity is neither interchangeable with
nucleotide similarity nor uniformly reflected in ViennaRNA-predicted structural
similarity. This exploratory analysis does not establish structural conservation,
RNA function, or orthology.

\subsection{Error analysis}
The dominant failure modes arise from limitations in projection, annotation, and
signal strength. Chain-based projection defines the search space and acts as a
hard constraint: if the corresponding region is not captured by genome alignments,
it cannot be recovered. Assembly gaps, base-level errors, and fragmentation can
remove or distort chain coverage and therefore propagate into missed or fragmented
predictions. Incomplete and imprecise reference annotations further
limit recoverable signal, particularly for lncRNAs. A substantial fraction of loci
exhibit weak or diffuse embedding signal, producing no detectable islands or only
fragmented matches. Overall, most observed failures are associated with missing or
ambiguous signal and projection constraints, rather than instability of the
downstream matching procedure.

\subsection{Runtime and scalability}
We evaluated \curia{} on the 19 genome-pair panel. On a single NVIDIA RTX PRO
4500 GPU, a full genome-pair run takes approximately three hours, with runtime
scaling approximately linearly with annotation size.
Embedding inference is performed on GPU with batched execution, while downstream
matching and alignment are CPU-bound; both reference and query island detection
are restricted to syntenically projected regions, substantially reducing the
effective search space.
