\begin{abstract}
Reference-based annotation transfer via orthology has been highly successful for
protein-coding genes but remains challenging for non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) due to
weak sequence conservation, ambiguous transcript boundaries, and incomplete
annotations. Here we present \curia{}, a pipeline for cross-species ncRNA
annotation that combines genome alignment chains with embedding-based
representations derived from the RiNALMo RNA foundation model. \curia{} formulates
cross-species ncRNA correspondence as synteny-constrained matching in embedding
space. It first identifies candidate loci using a simplified TOGA-based
projection, then detects localized regions whose embedding patterns resemble
those of annotated compact ncRNAs enriched for structured RNA classes
(``islands''), and finally establishes cross-species correspondence by
local alignment in embedding space. We applied \curia{} to 19 human-to-query
genome pairs. In the detailed human-to-mouse annotation comparison,
52.4\% of predicted short-ncRNA loci overlapped an annotated exon (any overlap).
For compact short ncRNAs, embedding similarity was largely redundant with the
tested conventional sequence metrics, whereas localized embedding-SW matching of
island regions provided additional ranking information beyond nucleotide
alignment, identity, k-mer similarity, composition, and length in the tested
human--mouse and human--cow samples.
From 34{,}846 annotated human lncRNA loci, a multi-species screen identified a
broad recurrent set of 1{,}693 annotated human lncRNA loci with an
embedding-supported region in at least
17 of 19 query genomes; 170 loci remained when every counted species was
additionally required to satisfy $d\leq0.02$. These
results suggest that combining synteny with embedding-based similarity can
complement conventional sequence conservation for cross-species ncRNA annotation
in settings characterized by weak or heterogeneous primary-sequence conservation,
establishing a proof of concept for integrating comparative genomics with RNA
foundation-model embeddings for genome-scale ncRNA analysis.
\end{abstract}
