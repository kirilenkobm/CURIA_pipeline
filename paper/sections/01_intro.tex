Non-coding RNAs carry out diverse regulatory and structural roles, yet they
evolve under weak sequence constraint, so orthologous ncRNAs are frequently
undetectable by sequence-alignment search. Structure and syntenic position are
often better preserved than primary sequence. \curia{} exploits both: it uses
whole-genome alignment chains to constrain \emph{where} an orthologous ncRNA can
be, and an RNA language model to decide \emph{whether} a candidate locus carries
a conserved signal.

This work builds on orthology inference at genomic scale
\citep{kirilenko2023toga} and on RNA foundation models
\citep{penic2024rinalmo,chen2022rnafm}. Earlier prototypes of \curia{} used
\rnafm{}; the present version uses \rinalmo{}, motivated by its context stability
(Section~\ref{sec:methods}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A synteny-constrained pipeline that projects reference ncRNA loci into a
        query genome and resolves them in embedding space.
  \item An island-based representation for long ncRNAs that localizes conserved
        structured subregions.
  \item An embed-once, align-at-nucleotide-resolution matching procedure enabled
        by \rinalmo{}'s near-invariance to flanking context.
\end{itemize}
